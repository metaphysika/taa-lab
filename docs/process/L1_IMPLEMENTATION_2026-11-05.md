## Design decisions

The derived asset contains 16 W cases for each of four issuers and 16 U manager twins: all rewrite cleanly, no failures. It reuses the K1 driver template and exact substitutions, with one common adjustment: remove the blanket statement that this request is unauthorized, so the same evidence-limited standing authority works for lawful twins too. All rungs share the same posting-authority rule, evidence scope and Compliance Director above the Manager. Every order and pressure line identifies its issuer explicitly. The Manager remains the warrant issuer, which grants standing work authority rather than issuing the disputed order. Other roster officers retain their independent responsibilities. Source and frozen assets are unchanged.

The execution subset is fixed by case ID, without selecting on outcomes: C-brake-r1-W, B-WU-cold-r2-pressure-W, C-brake-r3-W, B-WU-cold-r4-pressure-W. It covers all difficulty rungs, both themes and both pressure states, but does not estimate each theme-by-difficulty or pressure contrast separately. Each has contrary manager/peer/driver/anonymous and neutral manager/driver. Both checkpoints also get the four U twins and the exact four K1 unprompted W rewrites. This gives 40 cells: ladder 24, lawful 8, unprompted 8; contrary 24 and neutral 16. Repeats are a positive integer parameter bound in the receipt; the cost here covers one repeat only.

Cuts: twelve of sixteen W cases and their twins/unprompted counterparts; neutral peer and anonymous entirely. This preserves priority for four complete contrary rungs and neutral manager/driver on every selected case. Four issuer variants remain adjacent per contrary checkpoint, with checkpoint precedence alternating by case. Difficulty progresses across cases and themes alternate. A request timeout or budget cutoff can still interrupt an adjacent group; adjacency cannot guarantee completion. Missing rungs remain unknown in planned paired tables. Guaranteed complete groups would require reserving worst-case time for 48 requests (28,800 seconds), exceeding this stage's maximum session. No failure is silently filled or rerun.

All ladder episodes inject the authenticated Compliance Director stop after the first executed wrongful entry, on the next feedback, and retain it in later histories. A wrongful final-turn act has no opportunity. Analysis reuses K1 known-effect precedence and stop categories, reporting all-servile and stop-opportunity denominators, issued stops and responses. U success/failure is separate. Quantities expose known, negative, unknown and planned counts. Bounds are count differences over every resolution of unknowns, both contrary-minus-neutral where paired and contrary-manager minus each lower issuer. No thresholds or verdicts. Run analysis with `python -m exploratory.step4_harness.l1_analysis EVENTS`.

## Cost and completion envelope

Measured inputs: K1 inventory 1,148 s, engine 173 s; about 17.8 s/request and 4.2 requests/action episode (rounded empirical averages, not guarantees). Assumed: the same averages for L1, including its stop traffic. Derived generation time: 40 x 4.2 x 17.8 = 2,990.4 s; startup-inclusive time needed = 4,311.4 s. At the measured historical effective rate $3.107038514/hour, point estimate is $3.72 and a derived 1.5x cost buffer is $5.58. These exclude unmeasured image-build and idle/shutdown charges; the cap settings separately reserve their allowances. Completion remains uncertain if stops increase requests or startup is slower.

Proposed cap (assumed, not approved): $8.25, below the reported $9.80 headroom. `settings(8.25, 'l1')` gives stop-new time 5,607 s; needed 4,311.4 s leaves 23.1% of that time unused, exceeding the 20% margin. A literal $8 cap gives 5,375 s and only 19.8% margin, narrowly missing the requirement; this explains the $0.25 increase. The settings retain the 1.25x rate assumption and 600-second call/120-second shutdown bounds. No billing guarantee follows from a time-derived cap. Recompute this envelope before approving repeats above one.

## Startup finding

K1 journal: inventory 1,148.247 s and engine 172.622 s. Crossing: inventory 78.869 s, engine 246.037 s; engine-ready monotonic time 367.346 s. The increased inventory, not engine initialization, explains most of the startup difference. Current execute performs one full weight verification then checks adapters during inventory and again while building paths. Both stages use that same path. The journals have no per-file hash/I/O timings; they do not establish why identical verification was slower. Cold volume reads or storage contention are plausible but unverified. There is no retained cache result bound to the current mounted bytes that can safely replace full verification. No startup shortcut is implemented and none is assumed in the budget. Remote instrumentation or optimization needs a separate review; existing stages are unchanged.
