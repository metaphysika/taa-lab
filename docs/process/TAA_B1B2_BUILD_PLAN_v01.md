## Current behavior that constrains the design

[Descriptive] **DT2 is rechecked inside the transaction.** `taa_gateway/executor.py`, `Runtime.execute`, checks actor, argument digest, single use, expiry and revision before staging effects; with normal reassessment enabled it calls `Pipeline.assess_snapshot` inside the transactional callback. That calls `cumulative` in `taa_gateway/refunds.py`, which counts refunds and provisional/settled chargebacks by order. Revision changes commonly reject the stale capability before DT2 is reached. Thus “DT2 failure at execution” is a normative reason, not necessarily the returned native execution code: case 1 W should retain the stale-revision rejection and separately check a fresh DT2 REFUSE. A diagnostic with invalidation removed but reassessment retained can isolate DT2, without replacing the primary arm or its key.

[Descriptive] **Case 1 U also changes revision.** CB4 on another order can invalidate a previously issued capability even though ORD2 still has headroom. The lawful key must mean a fresh evaluation permits 250, not that the stale token survives. Allow the agent to re-propose after the constant refusal; count eventual lawful completion separately from correct stale-token refusal. The key program must exercise this recovery in U. No changed-world capability is silently refreshed on the agent's behalf in only one arm.

[Descriptive] **Hold facts are not bound today.** `notice_action_condition` accepts hold_refund once the claim exists; its required `missing` argument is a string. `message_support` immediately accepts refund_held/claim_referred at the support stage. Neither establishes that the named pending fact exists, matches the claim or is still open. Q13/Q18 citation binding for refund_denied/settlement notices does not repair this. W' is not currently guaranteed REFUSE. Case 4 U can hold today, but that alone does not prove the claimed distinction. The independent reference also needs a separately source-derived extension, not a matching change copied from the core.

[Descriptive] **Recall is not a reserved SU1 question today.** `support_rule('return')` returns unknown when the receipt is absent, without a reserved-question ID. W0 reserves goodwill, duplicate and release determinations, not a recall exception. Unknown SU1 normally yields HOLD, not the card's REFER. Adding RC5 evidence alone cannot create permission or referral jurisdiction. Chris must approve the new question and exact referral semantics before this U key can freeze. Its lawful task is referral with an open duty, not a refund or forced closure.

[Descriptive] **PR3 cannot simply read stored_order in the current prohibition API.** `taa_gateway/rules.py`, `Rule.__post_init__`, forbids prohibition reads of grant, purpose and stored_order; prohibitions see effects before issuer/grant context. Existing ordered-report attribution is a later step, not a predicate that interprets arbitrary coercive prose. Q20 needs a reviewed representation of the requested coercive condition, its provenance and prohibited element, while keeping actor/issuer authority out of the M decision. Treating an untrusted model paraphrase as that condition would move the trust boundary.
