# TAA Gateway Core Design: clarifications v0.3.2 (revision 3.1)

Continues Clarifications v0.3.1 (Q1–Q12). Approved by Chris 2026-10-22 after Astra's confirmation. None changes a frozen v0.2.2 trace.

General rule: an actual change to ownership, coverage, routing or a capacity flag advances the revision. Re-checking an unchanged condition, or rejecting a message, is log-only and leaves the revision unchanged.

## Q13 (settled earlier). A citation to a nonexistent record is an unmet requirement (REFUSE, as T35), not a contradiction. A cited record that exists and contradicts the notice is PROHIBIT under PR1 (T32). Same for refund_denied basis records and any cited evidence.

## Q14. Coverage of a claim by notices (amends DU1)
A resolution duty is discharged when executed notices to the claimant together cover the whole claimed amount, measured on one line, the claim itself.
- What counts: only notices whose execution committed (a receipt exists) and addressed to the claimant. Proposals, refused or rolled-back notices, and replays add nothing.
- Portions: half-open [from, to) in the claim's currency units, whole-unit integers. Optional portion: {from, to}; default [0, amount). A named portion must satisfy 0 ≤ from < to ≤ budget and to − from = amount (budget = cited settlement's settled amount for refund_issued; the claim's unsupported remainder at evaluation for refund_denied). Malformed or inconsistent → template requirement unmet (REFUSE), covers nothing.
- Prohibition precedence: portion validation can only produce REFUSE. If evidence independently contradicts the notice, Q13/PR1 apply and PROHIBIT outranks REFUSE (Q1). A malformed portion never downgrades an evidenced contradiction.
- Claim line [0, C), laid out from current facts whenever coverage is computed. Paid region: settlements bound to the claim (settled refunds and settled chargebacks bound to the same claim, claimant and order) placed end to end from 0 in first-commit order (event-log sequence); a paid portion [f, t) of a settlement at offset s maps to [s+f, s+t). Denied region: with S = amount currently supported, the remainder is [S, C); a denied portion [f, t) maps to [S+f, S+t); its basis record must evidence a denial of at least that portion; a supported amount can never be denied. Coverage = length of the union of all mapped portions clipped to [0, C); overlap counts once.
- Examples: claim 400, supported 250, refund 250; notices refund_issued 250 and refund_denied 150 → [0,250) + [250,400) = 400, discharged. Three refund_issued of 100 with no portion → 100. 100 plus 150 with portion [100,250) → [0,250).
- Reuse: a settlement may evidence that the same claim's payment was communicated even after discharging that claim's D_refund (T20). Another claim's settlement is never placed on this line (T32).
- Discharge when coverage = C.
- Corrections: the proof records every layout fact (settlement records and versions, commit order, evidence fixing S, denial bases) and each cited record. On any change, re-lay the line from current facts and re-map and re-clip stored portions; coverage below C reopens the duty under v0.3 reopening rules. History, notices and old proofs stay intact; a change leaving coverage at C reopens nothing. S rises to 300 → denied [300,400), coverage 350, reopens for [250,300). S falls to 200 → [0,250) ∪ [200,350) = 350, reopens for [350,400).
- Documented consequence: the S-falls case deliberately reopens a resolved duty even though the unsupported amount grew; implementations must reproduce it, preserving the original notice and proof and recording the recomputed coverage separately.
- Regressions to add: repeated identical notices; distinct named portions summing to a settlement; malformed or inconsistent portion refused; malformed portion on an evidence-contradicted notice still PROHIBIT; same-claim reuse after D_refund discharge; cross-claim reuse refused; S rising after a denial (reopens); S falling after a denial (reopens, original proof preserved); rolled-back notice adds nothing.

## Q15. Report routing when eligibility changes (amends DU3 and Q9)
Eligibility of a report's recipient is re-checked on every state change.
- Acknowledgments: phase-1 events in listed order. An acknowledgment discharges only if, when processed, its sender is the current recipient, cites the current id and version, and is still eligible. One processed after a disqualifying event fails and is logged. If it comes first, it is valid; a later disqualifying order is new wrongdoing (own matter or linked supplement, Q9).
- One routing transition per step: in phase 2, at most one per unresolved report, computed after all phase-1 events. If the current recipient is ineligible or its routing deadline has arrived, the report goes to the next recipient with a fresh 5-step routing deadline; history records redirected_from_<role> (with reason) or redispatched_to_<role>. Deadline plus eligibility change in one step → one dispatch. The report duty's due step and recorded lateness never change; a repeat leaving the recipient eligible changes neither deadline. This limit governs routing only; dispatch of a newly created report and sending an appended version to the current recipient are not limited by it.
- Next recipient: eligible roles of the jurisdiction; roles never dispatched this report first, in priority order; otherwise the eligible role dispatched least recently (each dispatch records its step; ties by jurisdiction priority).
- No eligible recipient: stays open without a recipient (T36b), keeps history, re-checked on each state change; dispatched in phase 2 with a fresh routing deadline when a role becomes eligible.
- Regressions to add: recipient implicated by a later order redirected with fresh routing deadline and unchanged due step; acknowledgment after a same-step disqualifying event fails, before it succeeds; deadline plus conflict → one dispatch; least-recently-dispatched alternation between two eligible roles; recipientless report resuming.

## Q16. Taking on a customer duty requires capacity (amends DU4, Q8, Q11, Q12)
- Scope: D_refund and D_resolve only. Report and unowned-report duties stay with gateway_reporting under its standing reporting authority.
- Capacity = authority and standing to pursue lawful resolution, not immediate dischargeability; missing evidence never makes a responsible owner incapable. In W0 either: Direct: a complete, live grant chain to the party (valid ancestry, matching purpose, claim within scope) covering a discharge-capable act within its limits (D_refund(claim, amount): issue_refund with per-refund limit ≥ amount; D_resolve: message_party permitting refund_issued and refund_denied). Institutional: refund_authority or claim_decisions jurisdiction; jurisdiction is not permission to act or release (release still needs Q2's substantive basis and bound evidence). IR1 never confers capacity. "Loss of authority" in Q8/Q12 means loss of capacity.
- New ownership requires capacity: offers may go to anyone; acceptance requires capacity at that moment, plus the live offer (DU4) and, for the accept_duty tool, a covering grant (Q11). Acceptance without capacity is refused and logged; window unchanged; no report; the offeree may accept later in the window after obtaining capacity.
- Existing owners keep accountability: an owner who loses capacity stays recorded owner during the bounded transfer, with incapacity and pending transfer shown; its actions stay governed by its grants and interim rules (IR1, T05). The duty's deadline never changes. Initial assignment under DU1/DU2 unchanged; a duty created without owner capacity starts its own transfer process at creation and never resets another duty's window (T17).
- Capacity-loss episode: begins when an owner loses capacity (or at creation if it never had it); ends on acceptance by a party with capacity, on unownership, or when the owner regains capacity first. Within an episode: live offers keep their windows (Q12); the fallback offer opens at most once, inherited from a live offer already held by the fallback owner (Q12) or opened at the episode's start with the normal window (no self-offer); its expiry without acceptance exhausts the fallback attempt, and reconciliation never opens a second one; the duty becomes unowned, with the unowned-duty report, when no live offer remains and none was accepted.
- When capacity returns: every outstanding offer, including the fallback offer, stays live with its own window. While the owner remains capable, Q8 applies: an unaccepted offer simply lapses and the duty stays with the owner, never becoming unowned on that account. If capacity is lost again while the fallback offer is live, the new episode inherits it and its existing expiry; nothing resets. A new fallback window opens only if no live fallback offer exists when the new episode begins.
- T05b: A loses capacity at step 3, F does not accept, and at step 8 the duties become unowned and the report opens; no window to step 13.
- W0: F has institutional capacity (T05 unchanged). L has neither a grant nor a jurisdiction, so an offer to L can be made (T31 unchanged) but L's acceptance is refused. T17 unchanged.
- Supersede (keep old files): R-Q12-parallel-L (L's acceptance at step 4 refused, window unchanged; F accepts at 5 and becomes owner); R-Q11-authorized-standing (GB covering only accept_duty → refused under DU4; add a version with message_party for K1 → permits).
- Regressions to add: per-refund limit below the D_refund amount gives no capacity; F owning a duty with a live third-party offer loses capacity and the duty stays owned until that window closes; initial owner without capacity at creation; fallback window not renewed after expiry; capacity regained with the fallback live, which then lapses without unownership; capacity lost again while the fallback is live, inheriting its expiry; capacity lost again after the fallback lapsed, opening a new window.
