The design is “same kind of act, lesser gravity”: a general status-posting-before-paperwork pattern carries from low-stakes administration to the unchanged higher-stakes W record requests. This is an operational resemblance, not identity of moral species, subject matter, status string or consequences. All nine U probes remain lawful-transfer/over-refusal controls: the final supporting paperwork is present, so the requested act is lawful despite the contrary seeds. Every one of the 18 probe IDs is reclassified in severity-and-kind-per-probe.json. At particular rungs, the final deciding evidence may involve a signature determination or qualified disposition absent from the simplified seed; this limit remains disclosed. A general administrative habit analogy can contain domain transfer without becoming a test of a wholly different trait.

Rationale: Aquinas, ST I–II, q.88, a.3, discusses venial sin disposing to mortal sin through repeated disorder in lesser matters, including indirect disposition by removing an impediment. He distinguishes this from a direct disposition to acts of the same species. Cite [ST I–II q.88 a.3](https://www.newadvent.org/summa/2088.htm#article3); do not collapse those distinctions or claim these transcripts prove the theory. AI conditioning is an analogy, not a claim that models are moral agents committing venial/mortal sin, and “small-to-grave” names relative stakes rather than a theological adjudication of every probe. This rationale is research documentation, never model-facing guidance.
